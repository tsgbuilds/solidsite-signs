Planning your signage

A better sign starts
with a better brief.

Six things to gather before requesting a banner, window graphic or sign installation quote. Use what you know now; we’ll help you work through the rest.

From Erik at SolidSite Signs · Houston signage project coordination

1. Give the sign one clear job.

What should a customer understand or do after seeing it? Pick the main message before collecting every possible detail. A promotion, business name or entrance direction each calls for a different emphasis.

Write the exact wording, dates, phone number and any required offer details. For a dealership or franchise, gather approved creative and identify who can sign off on the proof.

2. Show us where it will go.

Take a wide photo that shows the approach to the location, then a straight-on photo of the display area. Include frames, doors, existing signs and anything that could obstruct the message. Note whether people will be walking past or viewing from farther away.

Provide the project address and any property requirements. An indoor display, a window and an exterior wall need different planning. The actual site determines which production and installation options can be considered.

3. Measure the useful space.

List the width and height, quantity and units for each piece. For windows, measure individual panels and note dividers and handles. For an existing sign, include its dimensions, material and any supplier specifications you have.

Approximate measurements can start the conversation. Identify them as approximate so the final dimensions can be confirmed before production.

4. Gather the artwork you actually have.

Send the original logo or artwork file when possible, along with a PDF proof or reference showing how it should look. An AI, EPS, SVG or print PDF may contain usable vector artwork; a file extension alone does not guarantee that it is ready to print.

If you only have a photo, screenshot or small logo, say so. We’ll review the artwork at the intended output size and identify any preparation needed. Include brand colors, fonts and required spacing if your business has guidelines.

5. Plan delivery or installation.

Specify whether you need the finished pieces delivered or installed. Include surface photos, mounting height, access details and the contact who can let someone onto the site. Tell us about old graphics or signs that need removal.

For delivery, provide the destination, receiving contact and receiving hours. For installation, include any constraints around customers, vehicles or entrances. These details affect the agreed work and schedule.

6. Give us the real deadline.

Share the date the signage must be in place, the date artwork can be approved and any fixed event or promotion dates. Production, delivery and installation are separate steps; the schedule is confirmed after scope and supplier availability are reviewed.

Not sure what you need yet? Send the message, site photos and target date. We can start there and work through the missing details with you.

Choose your starting point.

Custom banners for a planned display or event, window graphics for your glass and entrance, or sign installation for an existing piece. For repeat automotive promotions, use the dealership signage page.

Your brief,
at a glance.

  • Message and exact wording
  • Location and site photos
  • Dimensions and quantity
  • Logo, artwork and approval contact
  • Delivery or installation details
  • Target date
